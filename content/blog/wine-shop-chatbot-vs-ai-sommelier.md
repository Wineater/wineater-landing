Wine shops hear two words for the same idea: chatbot and AI sommelier. They solve different problems, and the difference matters when you decide what to put on your site or on your shelf.

## What a chatbot does

A generic chatbot answers questions. Opening hours, delivery costs, return rules and order status are the typical jobs. Some chatbots also talk about wine, but they draw on whatever they learned in general, so they can name a bottle you have never stocked.

For a shop that is a problem. A shopper who reads "try a Chablis Premier Cru" and then cannot find it in your catalog leaves with nothing.

## What an AI sommelier does

An AI sommelier has one job: turn what a shopper wants into bottles you can sell. The shopper writes in plain words, for example "something for steak, under 30" or "like Barolo but cheaper". The sommelier returns a short list from the catalog of that shop, with a reason for each pick.

## Side by side

| | Generic chatbot | AI sommelier |
|---|---|---|
| Main job | Answer support questions | Recommend bottles |
| Where answers come from | General knowledge | Your catalog |
| Can name a wine you don't stock | Yes | No |
| Explains why a wine fits | Sometimes | Every pick |
| Knows your stock | Only if connected | Stock sync keeps it current |

## How to choose

Ask three questions.

1. Do most visitor questions concern orders and delivery? A support chatbot fits that.
2. Do visitors struggle to choose among 200 bottles? That calls for a sommelier.
3. Do you want to control which wines get recommended? Only a catalog-based tool lets you do that. In Wineater, a shop can promote selected products and they rank a little higher.

You can run both: a support bot for logistics and a sommelier for the choosing part.

## What to check before you buy any of them

- Does every recommendation come from your catalog?
- Does it respect stock?
- Does it explain each pick in your shop's language?
- Can a customer find the tool where they already browse: on the site, on a shelf QR code, or through an API?
